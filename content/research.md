### Working Papers

> #### [Multimargin Selection in Leniency Designs (Job Market Paper)](/uploads/JMP.pdf) {#multi-margin-selection-in-leniency-designs}
>
> <details class="paper-abstract">
> <summary>Abstract</summary>
> <p>Leniency designs exploit quasi-random assignment of cases to decision makers with different propensities to assign treatment. When decision makers weight unobserved case characteristics differently, a higher overall treatment rate can mask a lower probability of treating certain case types, with consequences for the interpretation of the IV estimand. I develop a model with finitely many latent regimes, each representing a case type with its own potential outcome distributions and decision-maker-specific treatment propensities. When each decision maker's treatment assignment within each regime and covariate cell preserves the marginal distribution of each potential outcome, observed outcome distributions admit a finite mixture representation. I show that pairwise Wald estimands weight regime-specific average treatment effects by each regime's relative contribution to the first stage. Because these weights can be negative, the IV estimand can be negative even when every regime's average treatment effect is positive. I provide conditions for identifying the number of regimes, regime-specific average treatment effects, and IV weights using either parametric restrictions on outcome distributions or auxiliary pretreatment measurements. I also characterize when the weights are nonnegative and when they are constant across decision-maker pairs, and develop pointwise asymptotically valid bootstrap tests of both restrictions for a fixed number of regimes. In an application to Miami-Dade County bail decisions, I find substantial variation across latent case types in the effects of pretrial release on sentence duration.</p>
> </details>

> #### [A Locally Robust Semiparametric Approach to Examiner IV Designs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19144) {#a-locally-robust-semiparametric-approach-to-examiner-iv-designs}
>
> **Status:** Reject and Resubmit, Journal of Econometrics
>
> **Award:** 2024 Outstanding Third Year Paper Prize
>
> <details class="paper-abstract">
> <summary>Abstract</summary>
> <p>I propose a locally robust semiparametric framework for estimating causal effects using examiner IV designs when adjustment for many or continuous covariates makes saturation infeasible or produces sparse cells. The key ingredient of this approach is an orthogonal moment function that removes the first-order effect of estimation errors in the two treatment regressions defining the examiner IV. I derive the orthogonal moment function and show that it remains valid under misspecification when, for each treatment regression, either that regression or the corresponding Riesz representer in the influence function adjustment is correctly specified. The proposed framework not only allows estimation of the examiner IV using a wide range of nonparametric and machine learning techniques, including LASSO, neural networks and random forests, but also delivers root-<em>n</em> consistent estimation and valid inference under suitable regularity conditions. I examine the finite-sample performance of the estimator through Monte Carlo simulations. I also apply the method to US patent examiners, using regularized regressions to account for differences in technology and other application characteristics.</p>
> </details>

> #### [Nonparametric Testability of Slutsky Symmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05603) {#nonparametric-testability-of-slutsky-symmetry}
>
> **Status:** Submitted
>
> **Coauthor:** Florian Gunsilius
>
> <details class="paper-abstract">
> <summary>Abstract</summary>
> <p>Economic theory implies strong limitations on what types of consumption behavior are considered rational. Rationality implies that the Slutsky matrix, which captures the substitution effects of compensated price changes on demand for different goods, is symmetric and negative semi-definite. While empirically informed versions of negative semi-definiteness have been shown to be nonparametrically testable, the analogous question for Slutsky symmetry has remained open. Recently, it has even been shown that the symmetry condition is not testable via the average Slutsky matrix, prompting conjectures about its non-testability. We settle this question by deriving nonparametric conditional quantile restrictions on observable data that permit construction of a fully nonparametric test for Slutsky symmetry in an empirical setting with individual heterogeneity and endogeneity. The theoretical contribution is a multivariate generalization of identification results for partial effects in nonseparable models without monotonicity, which is of independent interest. This result has implications for different areas in econometric theory, including nonparametric welfare analysis with individual heterogeneity for which, in the case of more than two goods, the symmetry condition introduces a nonlinear correction factor.</p>
> </details>
